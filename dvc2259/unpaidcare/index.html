Question:

At what age did people provide the most unpaid care to others (because of long-term physical or mental health conditions or illnesses, or problems related to old age)?

Correct!

People aged 50 to 59 years had the highest percentage providing unpaid care (because of long-term physical or mental health conditions or illnesses, or problems related to old age).

People in their 50s provided the most unpaid care (16%). With life expectancy in the UK continuing to rise – though at a slower pace in the last decade – it is increasingly likely that people in their 50s, 60s and 70s will have parents or other older relatives who might need care.

Overall, women take on a greater share of informal care than men. Almost 1 in 5 (19%) of females in their 50s provided unpaid care, compared to around 1 in 8 (12%) of males.

However, from the age of 80, males provided more unpaid care than females. Our Milestones: journeying through adulthood article explained that this may be because women in this age group are more likely than men to be widowed, so they no longer have a spouse to care for. Moreover, by this age, their parents are likely to have died. The article also revealed that men in these older age groups are likely to be caring for their (typically younger) spouse or partner.

Females aged below 80 years provided more unpaid care than males
